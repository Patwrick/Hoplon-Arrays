## Why custom terms

Creative Commons' NonCommercial condition considers the purpose of a use; an entity's for-profit status alone does not decide whether it complies. CC also recommends against applying its licenses to software. The owner's request excludes business use itself, including internal use, and covers CAD plus scripts, so this project uses explicitly labeled custom wording. [CC NonCommercial explanation](https://creativecommons.org/faq/#does-my-use-violate-the-noncommercial-clause-of-the-licenses), [CC software guidance](https://creativecommons.org/faq/#can-i-apply-a-creative-commons-license-to-software)

Business-use restrictions are incompatible with the Open Source Definition; describe the project as available for personal/noncommercial use, not open source. [Open Source Definition, section 6](https://opensource.org/osd)

## Legal limits and contribution review

A license cannot create rights its licensors do not hold. In particular, US copyright law limits protection of the mechanical/utilitarian aspects of useful articles; copyright in drawings does not automatically provide an exclusive manufacturing right. Consequently, this license alone does not guarantee control over every commercial use of the physical cable-management hardware. No patent or registered design right has been verified for this project. [U.S. Copyright Office: useful articles](https://www.copyright.gov/register/va-useful.html)

This is custom drafting, not a legal opinion or a guarantee of enforceability. Legal review is advisable before relying on it, particularly for restrictions concerning functional hardware. Before publication, confirm the authority to license every included contribution and preserve applicable third-party notices. Before accepting future contributions, confirm the contributor is entitled and willing to offer them under these terms; ownership is not automatically assigned to the maintainer. Publication, identity/privacy review and mechanical-validation gates remain separate from adding a license.
